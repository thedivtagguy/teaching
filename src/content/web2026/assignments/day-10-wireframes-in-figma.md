# Wireframes in Figma

Finish what you started in class today: **mid-fidelity wireframes for two pages of your personal site**, in Figma. Due Monday at the sprint's first standup. **Time-box: 90 minutes**; the rest of the weekend is yours.

## The rules

1. **Real content only.** Pull the actual words from your content inventory. Every box in your wireframe should contain (or clearly stand for) something that exists. Lorem ipsum is how layouts lie to you: a layout that only works with fake text doesn't work.
2. **Mid-fi means**: real text hierarchy (big, medium, small), gray boxes for images, your actual navigation labels from Day 8. No colors, no fonts, no styling seductions; your design tokens handle those, and they are already in your `global.css`.
3. **Hierarchy first.** Before you draw, decide: what is the one thing each page most wants a visitor to see? Give it the space that says so.
4. **Two pages**: your homepage, plus whichever of your three sections feels most alive.

## The self-check (before you call it done)

- **Squint test**: zoom out or blur your eyes. Does the important thing still dominate? If everything is the same size, nothing is important.
- **The stranger test**: would the one reader your manifesto names know where to click first?
- **The honesty test**: is there any box you can't name the real content for? Delete it or fill it.

## Submitting

Post a screenshot (or Figma share link) in your dev note, with one sentence: *"The first thing I want a visitor to see is ___."* If your wireframe doesn't make that sentence obviously true, that's your revision note for Monday morning.

Monday the sprint begins. You arrive with a live site, tokens, wireframes, and words, which means project week gets spent making the thing.
