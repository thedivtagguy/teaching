# Content Inventory

You have a site map. Every box on it is a page that needs words, and tonight and tomorrow night you write them. **No code.** This is due at the start of Day 10 (Friday), because Friday is design day and you design with real words or not at all.

You have two evenings for this. **Time-box: 90 minutes total.** If it takes longer, you are writing final prose, and final prose is not the assignment.

## What to produce

A single document (Obsidian, Google Doc, paper, anything) containing, for each page on your site map:

- **Homepage**: your name or title line, your one-sentence introduction (your manifesto has it in there somewhere, I promise), and your navigation labels exactly as you chose them in class.
- **Each of your three sections**: its title, a two or three sentence introduction, and the first *real entries*. An art journal needs its first two artworks picked and captioned. A learnings page needs three actual learnings. A playlist page needs one actual playlist.
- **About page**: your manifesto, edited however you like. This page is 80% written already; you are an editor tonight, not an author.

Bullet points are fine, and final prose is not required; the words just have to exist. "I'll write it when I build the page" is the lie that killed a hundred project weeks. During the sprint, every hour spent inventing content is an hour not spent building, and content invented at 4pm under deadline reads like it.

Per the [AI policy](/web2026/ai-policy): this is written work, so it is yours alone. No LLMs tonight.

## Why this works

The people who struggle most in project week are usually not fighting CSS; they are deciding *what to say* while trying to build the thing saying it. You have spent two days accidentally preparing: a wall of sticky notes (your subjects), three sections that passed the 15-minute test (your scope), a manifesto (your voice), a site map (your structure). Tonight just collects the harvest.

## Submitting

Post your inventory (or a link or screenshot of it) in your dev note before Friday's class. On Friday morning it becomes the raw material for your wireframes.
